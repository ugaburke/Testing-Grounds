## 3. Reddit Launch Post

**Target subreddit:** r/functionalprint

**Title:** I designed a drawer organizer with contoured utensil-profile slots — each slot is shaped like the utensil, not just a rectangle

**Body:**

I've been 3D printing kitchen organizers for a while now, and one thing always bugged me about drawer organizers: they're all just grids of rectangles.

A serving spoon and a peeler don't need the same slot shape. The serving spoon has a wide oval head and a narrow handle. The peeler is narrow throughout. When you put a serving spoon in a rectangular slot, it slides around because the slot is sized for the widest part, and the handle has nothing to rest against.

So I made an organizer with contoured slots. Each utensil type gets a 2D profile that matches its cross-section:

- **Serving spoon** — oval bowl section tapering to a narrow handle channel
- **Spatula** — wide flat head narrowing to a handle groove
- **Whisk** — egg-shaped bulb capturing the wire cage, tapering to handle
- **Tongs** — medium width throughout, slightly wider at the head
- **Ladle** — deep round bowl section + long narrow handle channel
- **Peeler** — slight blade widening + narrow handle

Think of it like a shadowboard in a workshop, but for your kitchen drawer.

The whole thing is parametric in OpenSCAD — enter your drawer's interior dimensions, configure how many of each utensil type you have, and it generates the layout. Front section is rectangular silverware (forks/knives/spoons), rear section is the contoured utensil slots.

Printed in PETG at 0.2mm, 15% infill. My 380mm × 500mm drawer took about 7.5 hours across two sections (had to split for my 256mm build plate). Tongue-and-groove joint at the split line — aligned it with a divider wall so you can't even see the seam.

The reaction from my wife: "Why doesn't everything come like this?"
